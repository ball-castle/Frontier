     数据赋能与圈层优化
——基于多源时空数据的青年职住分离治理决策案例

                   案例正文

  摘要：本案例选取杭州未来科技城为主案例、长沙麓谷高新区与梅溪湖新城为

对照，围绕数字产业集聚背景下青年就业、居住与通勤的空间错配展开。研究综合

运用手机信令、POI、交通流量等多源时空资料，并结合 226 份有效问卷、半结构化

访谈、多层 BLR—MLR 回归、双重差分模型以及 CIM+ABM 政策仿真，呈现规

划、住房、交通、公共服务、企业参与和数字治理工具依次进入治理现场的过程。

结果显示，杭州的高房价与就业集聚共同推动“核心就业—边缘居住”，长沙的低房

价减弱了住房约束，但产业园区公共服务与交通配套仍影响人才留存；通勤时间、

职住接近和轨道交通是通勤满意度的重要因素，新区开发在住房与服务跟进不足时

可能加剧职住分离。在设定的仿真情景下，住房与交通组合政策具有协同效应，数

字化匹配使综合治理指标相较基准情景进一步提高约 15%—20%。案例最终提出有

限财政、土地和协同能力下政策工具如何排序的开放决策。

关键词：职住分离；城市比较；数字化治理；多主体建模；政策组合

Abstract

This case examines youth job-housing separation in Hangzhou Future Sci-Tech City, with
Changsha High-Tech Zone and Meixi Lake New City as comparative cases. The study
combines multi-source spatiotemporal materials, including mobile signalling, points of
interest and traffic flows, with 226 valid questionnaires, semi-structured interviews,
multilevel BLR-MLR regressions, a difference-in-differences design, and a CIM+ABM
policy simulation. It traces how planning, housing, rail transit, public services, enterprise
participation and digital governance tools entered the policy process. The findings indicate
that high housing prices and concentrated employment jointly produce a core-
employment/peripheral-residence pattern in Hangzhou. Lower housing prices in Changsha
weaken the housing constraint, but inadequate public services and transport around
industrial parks still affect talent retention. Commuting time, job-housing proximity and
rail transit are important determinants of commuting satisfaction, while new-area
development may intensify separation when housing and services lag behind employment
growth. Under the specified simulation settings, combined housing and transport policies
generate a synergy effect, and digital matching further improves the composite governance
indicator by approximately 15%-20% relative to the baseline. The case ends with an open
decision on how to sequence policy instruments under fiscal, land and coordination
constraints.

Keywords: job-housing separation; urban comparison; digital governance; agent-based
modelling; policy mix
